Research Load Model

A business school decides teaching loads partly on research output. This turns that into something you can argue with: every faculty member is a dot, placed by weighted publication points over a rolling six-year window, inside the load band their score earns. Move the weights or the cutoffs and the room re-sorts itself.

Where the faculty land

The faculty on this page are not real. Departments and journals are: those are public facts about a college and worth seeing in place. Every person here is generated, and no real individual’s record is copied, sampled, or recoverable from anything in this repository. The article counts against each journal are synthetic too. The point is the instrument, not the numbers: clone it, point it at your own data, and the shape of your own college appears.
  • The unrated weight is the whole argument. Most business-school output sits in journals no rating list covers. Whatever you set “Unrated” to decides who earns a release, and at 1 point a high-volume author in unrated outlets outscores a colleague with two A-level papers. Every other control on this page is a detail next to that one.
  • Unrated is not one thing. It mixes practitioner outlets, journals a rating list simply does not cover, and journals whose name in a CV was too imprecise to match. A model that gives them all the same number is making a claim it cannot support. The house list is one way out: name the unrated journals your faculty actually publish in and score those separately.
  • Two questions, not one. Staying Scholarly Academic and earning a course release are different tests. “Unrated counts for SA, earns no points” splits them, and the bands move sharply when you do.
  • In-press items have no year. Accepted and forthcoming articles carry no publication date, so a model either drops them or dates them to the current year. Dropping them penalises exactly the people with work in the pipeline. Dating them assumes nothing slips. This build dates them and marks them with a raised number in the table.
  • A dot is a person. Codes rather than names still leaves department, rank, and an exact publication profile on screen, which is enough for a colleague to identify several people in a minute. Treat any build carrying real records as personnel material, keep it out of version control, and think about who is in the room before you show it.
